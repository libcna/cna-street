# Asset attribution

**The street is generated. Sixty-nine models, fourteen surfaces and eight people are not.**

That first sentence has been rewritten twice, and the honest version is the
current one: every mesh of the street itself, every sign, every letter, every
marking, every pane of glass and every weathering decal is generated by code
in this repository from a seed -- and the props standing in it, the trees
nearest the showcase viewpoints, the cars parked in the hero bays, the people
on the footway, the hero cafe's fittings and the fourteen surfaces the camera
gets closest to are third-party work with a clean licence, tracked one file
at a time. There is no third-party font and no sound file, and the build's default
path needs no network.

## The record

`assets/external/manifest.json` is machine-readable so that a validation step
can check it rather than a person having to. Per model it carries the local
name, the original title, the author, the copyright line, the source URL and
the repository it came from, the exact licence and its URL, whether attribution
is required, whether redistribution is allowed, the retrieval date, the
original format, every file with its SHA-256 and byte count, what was done to
it, and what it is for in the scene. Per surface it carries the same, plus the
scan's physical size in metres and the tile size the geometry lays it at, so
the content build can write the UV scale that keeps a brick the size of a
brick.

`scripts/validate-assets.py` checks all of it -- and is registered with CTest,
so a manifest that drifts fails the test suite. `scripts/fetch-assets.sh`
fetches every declared file and verifies it before it is used. The files are
**fetched, not committed**: the set runs to some 300 MB and putting somebody
else's work into a repository's permanent history is a separate permission
from using it. A checkout that has not run the script has generated surfaces
and generated props everywhere, and every scanned thing has a generated
stand-in.

## Where they come from

Four sources, each chosen because the licence of every item is stated per
item by its publisher and is one this repository can carry:

* **[KhronosGroup/glTF-Sample-Assets](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Sample-Assets)**
  -- sixteen models, the props behind the shop glass and the imported-rig
  round trip. The licence recorded for each is the one that repository states
  for *that model* in its own `Models.md`, not the repository's overall terms,
  because the same repository carries models this project cannot use:

  | Rejected | Licence | Why not |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Duck | SCEA Shared Source License 1.0 | Not compatible with this repository's MIT terms |
  | Sponza | CRYENGINE Limited Licence Agreement | Redistribution restricted |
  | Virtual City | 3DRT, testing use only | Not licensed for use in a published work |

* **[Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com)** -- twenty models and fourteen PBR
  texture sets, all CC0-1.0, which is the licence Poly Haven publishes its
  whole library under. The models are photogrammetry scans of street
  furniture and clutter: a fire hydrant, two utility cabinets, a bench, a
  cafe table and chairs, an A-board, planters and shrubs, crates and cartons,
  refuse sacks, manhole covers, a car under a cover, and a small tree cut to
  two street-weight levels of detail in Blender by `scripts/blender-tree-lod.py`.
  The surfaces are the asphalt, the paving slabs, the granite kerb, three
  bricks, the lime render, the sandstone ashlar, the concrete panels, the roof
  tiles, the plane-tree bark and the shop floor, each resampled and repacked
  by `scripts/prepare-surfaces.py` into the catalogue's albedo / normal / ORM
  form under the name of the generated surface it replaces.

* **[Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com), through the
  [Objaverse](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/objaverse) mirror** --
  eight cars, every one CC-BY-4.0. Poly Haven has no vehicles and no other
  CC0 source has a realistic one, so the fourth pass went to Sketchfab, whose
  authors choose a Creative Commons licence per model and whose download
  embeds the author, the licence and the page in the file's own metadata
  (`asset.extras`); the manifest records what the file says about itself,
  and the eight were chosen from authors publishing their own modelling
  rather than conversions. The files are fetched from Objaverse, which
  redistributes Sketchfab's CC-licensed models under their own licences,
  because Sketchfab's own download needs an account. Each is normalised in
  Blender by `scripts/blender-vehicles.py` -- backdrop dropped, faced +Z,
  scaled to the real car's length, one mesh per material, a far level of
  detail -- and the attribution the licence asks for is below.

* **[MakeHuman](https://static.makehumancommunity.org)** -- the base mesh,
  its targets and the system asset pack (skins, clothes, shoes, hair, eyes,
  eyebrows), released to CC0 in 2020. The eight people in the crowd are
  built from it in Blender by `scripts/blender-people.py` with MPFB, the
  MakeHuman extension for Blender, which is GPL software the way Blender is:
  a tool that is run, never linked, and nothing it produces carries its
  licence. The tool is declared in the manifest's `tools` list and fetched
  and verified like an asset, so the people can be rebuilt from nothing.

## Attribution

Generated from the manifest by `scripts/attribution-table.py` rather than
typed, so the two cannot drift apart. CC0-1.0 requires no attribution and it
is given anyway; CC-BY-4.0 requires it and this is it.

## Why the rest is generated

That is a deliberate architectural choice rather than a way of avoiding the
question. A generated surface can be produced at any resolution, tiles by
construction, carries a physically sensible roughness map instead of a guessed
one, and — because it comes from a seed — is byte-identical on every machine
that builds the repository. The cost is that each one has to be *authored*:
`street/src/Assets/TextureFactory.cpp` is a description of what clay pantile,
car paint and packaging actually look like, not a call into a noise library.
Where a scan of the real thing exists under a clean licence and is visibly
better, the scan is used; where the thing cannot be scanned -- a shop's
lettering, a road marking, a weathering stain placed where the water runs --
it is generated.

## A note on the traffic signs

The sign faces are **original designs in the visual language of continental
European signage**. The shape-and-colour grammar they follow — a red-ringed
white disc prohibits, a red-bordered white triangle warns, a blue rectangle
informs — is a functional convention rather than anyone's artwork, and it is
what makes a sign readable at a glance. The pictograms inside them are drawn in
`SignFactory` rather than traced from any jurisdiction's official artwork, which
would bring that jurisdiction's licence with it. Nothing here reproduces a
specific real sign, a specific real street, or any real organisation's marks.

The street names, shop names and vehicle registration on the plates are
invented.

## If an external asset is ever added

It goes in the manifest with its name, title, author, copyright, source,
licence, every file's digest, what was done to it and what it is for, and its
licence has to be CC0, public domain, or a permissive licence that allows
redistribution in this repository. CC-BY needs the attribution reproduced
here. Assets with unknown licences, and assets extracted from commercial
games, are not acceptable at any quality. `scripts/validate-assets.py` refuses
an undeclared file and an unlisted licence, and CTest runs it.
